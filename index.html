TracePermit is a model-neutral governance layer for AI agents operating inside private knowledge systems. It records the sources an agent consumes, traces the evidence behind its claims, and blocks, quarantines, or escalates output with missing, altered, or untrusted provenance. It catches unsupported claims — not false ones. A fabrication that cites a real, unaltered document still passes the gate; that boundary is published below, not buried.

Kill unsupported claims in the sandbox. Before they corrupt your system.

02

The moat: catch it before it contaminates the build

Real agent systems chain many models — extract, retrieve, analyze, draft, summarize. One unsupported claim upstream silently becomes a premise downstream and poisons the shared build. The Overseer — TracePermit's verdict engine — sits at the gate between every agent and operational memory: a claim that reconstructs to evidence is admitted; a claim that doesn't is quarantined in a sandbox and a human is alerted. Press run — one agent is hallucinating.

05

The boundary I publish

A verification system is only as trustworthy as its stated failure modes. These are the measured limits — the part most demos leave out, and the part a research team should care about most.

Negative result · reported, not hidden

Provenance is not semantic support

Does structural grounding catch hallucinations in real model prose?
0.53

Balanced accuracy on 14,289 human-annotated hallucination spans (RAGTruth). Faithful paraphrase introduces tokens absent from the source exactly as fabrication does — so byte-level grounding can't separate them. Semantic entailment is a layer above the substrate, and I say so.

Is a content address confidential?
26 ms

Time to recover a date of birth from its published content id by dictionary attack. Content addressing gives integrity, not confidentiality — low-entropy evidence needs a keyed commitment. Measured, and stated as a limitation.
